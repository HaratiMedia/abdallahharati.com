Biography

Bringing Brazilian Music to The World

No matter where he is, Munich, Germany-based guitarist, producer, and composer Abdallah Harati always brings his beloved birthplace of Brazil with him. His transporting and dazzling musicality welcomes listeners from around the world deep into the soul of Brazil.

It's a cultural connection that harkens back to his childhood. The São Paulo, Brazil-born artist recalls: “I remember hearing my grandfather playing guitar behind my grandmother's singing, and just feeling their joy. At the time, I was studying classical music and it just didn't bring me any happiness,” Abdallah recalls. “I wanted that feeling my grandpa had of playing music that really resonated.” He continues: “I had a brief rebellion playing rock n' roll—one year I even won a festival—but a voice inside me urged me to wake up and bring Brazilian music to the world.”

Abdallah plays the sweetly sonorous nylon string guitar, and he cites as influences Pixinguinha, Garoto, Dilermando Reis, Baden Powell, Raphael Rabello, Hermeto Pascoal, Egberto Gismonti and the Brazilian martial art Capoeira. He has synthesized a singular sensibility by fusing the traditions of choro, samba, Afro-Brazilian, capoeira, Brazilian jazz, and American jazz. Abdallah's repertoire consists of a mix of original compositions, and Brazilian traditionals and Brazilian jazz standards. He has the ability to make one guitar sound like three with intricate chordal passages, lyrical flights of melodic fancy, and propulsive rhythmic guitar grooves. He often sounds like a Brazilian Joe Pass. Since 2020, Abdallah has released 29 independent singles and EPs under his own Harati Media label, now totaling over 1.02 million streams across all platforms. His recordings have been featured in tastemaking outlets like All About Jazz, and he has organically earned placements on Spotify editorial playlists, including “Relaxing Spanish Guitar” and “Violão Brasileiro.”

The Background

His talent for playing by ear has granted him entry to a wide variety of musical opportunities, and Abdallah has performed, recorded, and produced throughout Europe and beyond. Select career highlights include being part of Alvaro Carrilho's ensemble; opening for artists such as Altamiro Carrilho, Oswaldo Montenegro and Tom Zé; being awarded “Best Solo Guitar” performer at the USP Music Festival in 1994 and 1995; and being part of the esteemed Choro Club in São José dos Campos. He was also part of one of the most important Choro initiatives, Escola Portátil de Música, led by Luciana Rabello, Maurício Carrilho, Pedro Amorim, Álvaro Carrilho and Celsinho Silva. Abdallah remains active producing other independent musicians and artists under the Harati Media label, and several of his collaboration videos with remote musicians around the world have become buzzed-about in the virtual environment.

More recently, Abdallah formed Caiana Duo with percussionist João Araújo, pairing his 7-string guitar with pandeiro and hand percussion in a Choro, Samba and Brazilian jazz dialogue that blends original compositions with reworked pieces by Pixinguinha, João Pernambuco, Heitor Villa-Lobos and Baden Powell. The duo has performed at the International Choro Festival Múnchen, a solidarity concert at Amerikahaus, and repeat dates at Seidlvilla, and released the singles “Viajeiro” and “Bebê” — the latter a Baíao arrangement of a Hermeto Pascoal composition. Solo, Abdallah's recent catalog includes the singles “Prepara o Arame” and “Tuas Mãos.”

Outside of music, Abdallah is a techie in the business world who has worked successfully in corporate settings for over 25 years. He is an electronic engineer with a degree from São Paulo, Brazil's Bauru College of Engineering. “My business background has helped me be self-sufficient as a working musician, informing how I read contracts. My technical background has helped me in learning how to use recording equipment. In turn, my music career has informed my business strategist work, inspiring me to think outside the box,” Abdallah shares.

Abdallah grew up in São José dos Campos, and, since 2015, has been established in Munich, Germany. Prior to that, Abdallah lived in Guatemala City, Mexico City, San Juan, Dallas, Stockholm, Dubai, Hanoi and Nairobi. He studied music formally at Berklee College of Music in Boston, and at Carmen de Las Cuevas in Granada, Spain — and is currently back at Berklee, pursuing a Master's in Music Business. Perhaps his greatest schooling was at home, where many generations of his family were musicians—some amateurs, some professionals—including his grandparents, his mother, his uncle, and cousins. Abdallah's musical trajectory began with the flute, then the piano, before his regimented study of classical guitar. His music has also been shaped by Capoeira, which he has practiced since he was a teenager; this tradition's Afro-Brazilian roots and its relationship with instruments such as the berimbau and the congas have made an indelible impact on his sound. He was eventually seduced away from classical music through the irresistible home influence of Choro, Seresta, and Samba.

Though he was actively performing and sharpening his chops for years beforehand, it was the pandemic that spurred Abdallah to share his musical gifts globally, beginning a monthly series of independently produced singles that has continued ever since — old-school outings tracked and produced by Abdallah himself, using only real instruments.

Select standouts in Abdallah's catalog include “Caçador,” “Grão de Areia,” “15 de Fevereiro,” and “Se Ela Perguntar.” The breathtaking “Caçador” is a traditional Afro-Brazilian religious song related to the forest and to Indigenous Brazil (Caçador means hunter in Portuguese). “Grão de Areia” features sensual female vocals, the single-string Brazilian percussion instrument the berimbau, and a lively rhythmic bed of tambourine and congas, with stunning interludes where Abdallah's rhythm guitar deftly dialogues with the percussion. The balmy “15 de Fevereiro” is an original, flaunting virtuosic melodies often doubled and complemented by saxophone and flute.

Abdallah turns in an effortlessly masterful version of “Se Ela Perguntar,” a composition by Dilermando Reis. It is a spacious solo guitar piece with impressionistic chordal passages punctuated by melodies and flurries of classically flavored single-note runs, all played with grace and restraint. With 242,015 Spotify streams to date it remains one of Abdallah's most popular recordings — second only to “Sons de Carrilhões,” from the same session, now his most-streamed track at 334,078 streams.

In six years, Abdallah has built a platform for the lifetime of music he's been nurturing. “It's been such a gift to be able to combine my work in the tech realm, in terms of recording and production, with my lifelong love and passion for Brazilian music. And I will always be loyal to the music of my home—it's my true love,” he says.

The Moment

Six years into releasing independently, Abdallah is working across two active projects at once. As a soloist, he continues the monthly-release discipline that built his catalog, most recently with “Prepara o Arame” and “Tuas Mãos.” Alongside that, Caiana Duo — his partnership with percussionist João Araújo — has become the more visible stage project, with regular concerts around Munich and a growing single catalog anchored by “Viajeiro” and “Bebê.”

He's also folding formal study back into the work: a Master's in Music Business at Berklee College of Music, taken one course at a time, feeding directly back into how Harati Media — his label and production house — is run. The throughline hasn't changed since the pandemic-era singles that started this: real instruments, self-produced, released on a schedule, and always carrying Brazil with him.
